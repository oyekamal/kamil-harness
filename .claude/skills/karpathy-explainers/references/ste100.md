# Rung 1: ASD-STE100 writing, "80% of the way"

ASD-STE100 (Simplified Technical English) is the controlled language of aircraft maintenance
manuals. It has two parts: writing rules and a dictionary of about 900 approved words, each with
one approved meaning. Karpathy's point: models know it well, and its limits make output much
easier to read. His caveat: ask for "80% of the way". Full compliance sounds stilted for software
and science, because the dictionary has no words for most of our concepts.

The STE maintainers also warn that AI text can *look* STE while it breaks the rules. So do not
trust the style by eye. Run `scripts/ste_lint.py`, then fix what it reports.

## The rules this skill applies (the 80% profile)

| # | Rule (STE section) | What to do | Lint |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | One word, one meaning (1.1–1.5) | Pick ONE term per concept in a glossary. Use it every time. Never swap in a synonym for variety. | ERROR via `--glossary` |
| 2 | Technical names are allowed (1.5) | Domain terms ("reaction quotient", "KV cache") are fine. Define each one the first time. | manual |
| 3 | Procedural sentences ≤ 20 words (5.1) | Steps, instructions, "do this". | ERROR |
| 4 | Descriptive sentences ≤ 25 words (6.1) | Explanations, facts. | ERROR |
| 5 | One instruction per sentence (5.2) | "Set Q to 10. Read the cell potential." Not "Set Q to 10 and read...". | warn |
| 6 | Paragraph ≤ 6 sentences, one topic (6.4) | One idea per paragraph. Put the topic sentence first. | ERROR |
| 7 | Active voice (3.7) | Say who does the action. "The anode releases electrons", not "electrons are released". | warn |
| 8 | Simple verb tenses (3.1–3.5) | Present, past, future, imperative. Avoid -ing verb forms ("by increasing" → "when you increase"). | warn |
| 9 | Keep the articles (2.3) | Write "the", "a", "an". Do not write telegraph style ("Set Q, read E"). | manual |
| 10 | Noun clusters ≤ 3 words (2.2) | "standard reduction potential table" → "the table of standard reduction potentials". | warn |
| 11 | Commands start with the verb (5.4) | "Increase Q." Warnings: the command first, then the reason. | manual |
| 12 | No contractions, slang, hedges | "do not", not "don't". Cut "basically", "just", "really", "simply", "obviously". | ERROR / warn |
| 13 | Use lists and tables for parallel items (5.x) | A vertical list for steps, a table for comparisons. | manual |
| 14 | Simple words over complex ones | use not utilize, start not commence, show not demonstrate, about not approximately, help not facilitate, find not ascertain, more not additional, before not prior to, then not subsequently. | warn |

STE approved verbs to prefer (sample, not the full dictionary): be, have, do, make, use, show,
give, get, find, keep, put, set, move, add, remove, increase, decrease, start, stop, change,
compare, measure, calculate, connect, flow, cause, go, come, push, pull, hold, release, apply,
let, read, write, look, try, occur, become, contain, include, refer.

## The render-step rule (most important)

Style instructions applied while the model *reasons* can bend the reasoning itself. So:

1. **Think in normal prose first.** Write the fact sheet (see SKILL.md step 1): the correct
   equation, symbols, units, sign rules, conditions, worked numbers, all computed in code.
2. **Then render** the fact sheet into STE. Rendering may cut words, never change a fact.
3. **Diff facts after rendering.** Every number, sign and direction in the STE text must match
   the fact sheet. If rendering dropped a condition ("at 25 °C", "for dilute solutions"), put it back.

## Shape of a good rung-1 explanation

```
**What it is.** 1–2 descriptive sentences. Name the thing and what it computes.
**The equation.** The formula, then one line per symbol: symbol, meaning, unit.
**What it tells you.** The one direction rule ("When Q increases, E decreases.").
**How to use it.** A numbered procedure, ≤ 20 words per step, one action per step.
**Worked example.** Numbers in, numbers out, units on every number.
**Limits.** When the formula stops being accurate, in 1–3 sentences.
**Glossary.** The one-term-per-concept list you used.
```

## Before / after

Before (35 words, passive, hedge, two synonyms for one quantity):
> Basically, the voltage of the cell is reduced as the reaction proceeds, since the emf depends
> on the ratio of products to reactants, which is being increased as the reactants are used up.

After (4 sentences, max 12 words, one term):
> The cell potential depends on the reaction quotient, Q. Q is the ratio of products to
> reactants. As the cell runs, Q increases. When Q increases, the cell potential decreases.

## Glossary file (feeds the lint)

```json
{"cell potential": ["voltage", "emf", "cell voltage", "EMF"],
 "reaction quotient": ["concentration ratio", "the ratio Q"],
 "electrons transferred": ["electron number", "charge number"]}
```

Run: `python3 scripts/ste_lint.py rung1.md --glossary glossary.json`. PASS means 0 errors and
warnings on at most 20% of sentences. That is the "80% of the way" bar.
The lint is a heuristic. It cannot check the approved dictionary, articles or meaning. Read the
text once more for those.
